# Security and compliance

## Pilot release controls

- Verify OIDC and RLS acceptance before any merchant feature release. Use distinct non-owner, no-bypass identity and merchant logins, tenant-local transactions and active membership/role checks. Legacy webhook and worker access needs a separate credential boundary and limited cross-tenant operations. Expand grants per feature and test that foreign rows and missing context fail closed.
- Keep OIDC redirects fixed, validate discovery, issuer, audience, nonce and PKCE, consume browser-bound state once, rotate/revoke digest-backed sessions and enforce exact origin and CSRF on mutations. Rate-limit login, callbacks, API and webhook ingress. Store secrets in managed configuration. Verify TLS and prevent configuration/logging from exposing credentials.
- Verify webhook authentication on raw provider evidence, bind by verified receiving asset, limit size/rate and persist before acknowledgement. Encrypt provider credentials with per-tenant context and versioned KMS keys. Rotation supports new versions, short overlap and rollback. Revoke old versions and fence queued work on disconnect or suspension.
- Treat customer content, provider errors and retrieved text as untrusted. Grant tools per agent, role, tenant and active consent. Validate input/output, separate approvals, redact logs, and cap AI rounds, spend, media size and outbound rates. Keep immutable actor/intent audit trails for connection, role, approval, publish, takeover, consent, payment state and replay.
- Minimize PII. Record purpose and lawful basis per processing activity, plus separate opt-in evidence where channel or marketing policy requires it. Default proposals are 90 days for message content and summaries and 365 days for redacted audit events, subject to merchant/legal review. Implement scoped export, deletion, cache/chunk/file purge, backup expiry, and access auditing before paid use. Limit model provider retention and secondary use under the contract/configuration.
- Establish provider and tenant incident controls. Pause affected channel, business or agent, rotate credentials, preserve redacted incident evidence, notify the right operator, reconcile and resume with sign-off. Exercise restore and rollback before pilot. Add richer forensic automation, periodic access review workflows and customer-facing compliance exports later.

## Nigerian privacy review

Map merchant/Kleva controller and processor responsibilities per activity, including Kleva's own identity, billing and product analytics. Execute data processing terms and list subprocessors. Provide accessible notices and a data subject request path. Assess controller/processor-of-major-importance classification, registration and DPO or DPIA obligations with Nigerian counsel. Document cross-border safeguards and cloud/AI locations before using foreign processors. Under section 40, plan processor-to-controller escalation on awareness, Commission notification by the controller within 72 hours of awareness where a breach is likely to risk individuals’ rights and freedoms, and immediate communication to affected individuals where high risk triggers that duty. Confirm recordkeeping, customer-facing notices, opt-outs and sector-specific requirements before launch. Verify applicability rather than treating all merchants and all incidents identically. [Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023](https://cert.gov.ng/ngcert/resources/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf) · [NDPC final General Application and Implementation Directive](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NDP-ACT-GAID-2025-MARCH-20TH.pdf).

For channel-specific constraints, review current terms per market and asset. Keep the assistant clearly identified as AI, honor human escalation and opt-outs, and use approved templates only with authorized purpose and consent. Confirm paid pilot eligibility against the applicable WhatsApp terms and app/account settings.
